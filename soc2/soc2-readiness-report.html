1. Purpose

This readiness documentation has been prepared to evaluate Ramdoot Solutions' current state of preparedness for a SOC 2 examination against the AICPA Trust Services Criteria (2017, updated 2022). The document serves three primary objectives:

  1. Baseline assessment: Establish a clear understanding of the controls currently in place across the organization, mapped to the Common Criteria (CC1 through CC9) and any additional Trust Services Categories that may be in scope.
  2. Gap identification: Identify areas where controls are absent, partially implemented, or insufficiently documented to meet the expectations of an independent auditor performing a SOC 2 Type I or Type II examination.
  3. Remediation planning: Provide a structured roadmap with prioritized actions that Ramdoot Solutions' management can execute to achieve readiness before engaging an external audit firm.

Important: This document is an internal working artifact. It does not constitute a SOC 2 attestation, report, or certification. SOC 2 examinations are performed exclusively by independent CPA firms licensed under AICPA standards.

3. Background — AICPA Trust Services Criteria

3.1 Trust Services Criteria Overview

The AICPA Trust Services Criteria (TSC), originally published in 2017 and most recently updated in 2022, provide the authoritative framework against which SOC 2 examinations are conducted. The TSC is organized around the COSO 2013 Internal Control framework and comprises the following Common Criteria families:

CC FamilyNameFocus Area
CC1Control EnvironmentTone at the top, governance, organizational structure, integrity, and ethical values
CC2Communication & InformationInternal and external communication of policies, objectives, and responsibilities
CC3Risk AssessmentIdentification and analysis of risks to achieving objectives; fraud risk
CC4Monitoring ActivitiesOngoing and periodic evaluation of control effectiveness
CC5Control ActivitiesPolicies and procedures that enforce management directives; segregation of duties
CC6Logical & Physical Access ControlsAuthentication, authorization, encryption, physical security
CC7System OperationsDetection of anomalies, incident response, disaster recovery
CC8Change ManagementChange authorization, testing, and deployment controls
CC9Risk MitigationVendor management, insurance, business continuity

Beyond the Common Criteria (which map to the Security category), the TSC defines four additional categories that may be included in the scope of a SOC 2 report: Availability, Confidentiality, Processing Integrity, and Privacy.

3.2 SOC 2 Type I vs Type II

AspectType IType II
Objective Evaluate the design (suitability) of controls at a specific point in time Evaluate both the design and operating effectiveness of controls over a defined period
Observation Period Single date (snapshot) Typically 6 to 12 months of continuous operation
Evidence Required Documented policies, procedures, system configurations at the point-in-time All Type I evidence plus operational logs, tickets, review records, and sample testing
Typical Use Case First-time assessment to demonstrate control framework is in place Ongoing assurance to clients and stakeholders; usually follows a Type I
Recommended For Organizations new to SOC 2 seeking initial attestation Organizations with mature, consistently operating controls

Recommendation: For organizations undergoing their first SOC 2 examination, beginning with a Type I assessment is common practice. This validates the control design before committing to the extended observation period required for Type II.

11. Management Responsibilities

Under the AICPA standards governing SOC 2 examinations, management of the service organization (Ramdoot Solutions) bears specific responsibilities that cannot be delegated to the auditor:

  1. System description: Management is responsible for preparing and presenting the system description fairly, in accordance with the description criteria. The auditor does not draft this description; management owns it.
  2. Control design and operation: Management is responsible for designing, implementing, and operating controls that meet the applicable Trust Services Criteria.
  3. Assertion: Management must provide a written assertion that the system description is fairly presented, controls are suitably designed, and (for Type II) that controls operated effectively during the examination period.
  4. Completeness of information: Management must provide complete and accurate information to the auditor, including all known control deficiencies and incidents.
  5. Subservice organization oversight: Where carve-out or inclusive methods are used for subservice organizations, management must maintain appropriate oversight and monitoring.
