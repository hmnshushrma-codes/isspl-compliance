1. Purpose

This procedure provides a structured, repeatable approach for detecting, reporting, triaging, containing, eradicating, and recovering from security incidents at Ramdoot Solutions. It ensures that incidents are handled consistently, impact is minimized, evidence is preserved for investigation, and regulatory notification obligations — including CERT-In's 6-hour reporting requirement for cyber incidents — are met.

2. Scope

This procedure applies to all information security incidents affecting Ramdoot Solutions, including but not limited to:

  • Unauthorized access to systems, applications, or data.
  • Malware infections, ransomware attacks, and phishing compromises.
  • Data breaches or suspected data exfiltration.
  • Denial-of-service attacks.
  • Insider threats and policy violations with security implications.
  • Physical security breaches affecting information assets.
  • Third-party/vendor security incidents affecting Ramdoot Solutions data.

This covers all employees, contractors, systems, and environments (production, staging, corporate).

5. Prerequisites

  • CSIRT members are designated and trained, with current contact information maintained.
  • SIEM platform is operational and receiving logs from all critical systems.
  • Incident response communication channels (dedicated Slack/Teams channel, bridge line) are established.
  • Forensic toolkit (disk imaging tools, network capture tools, chain-of-custody forms) is available.
  • CERT-In reporting templates and contact information are documented and accessible.
  • All employees have completed security awareness training covering incident reporting.

6. Step-by-Step Procedure

Step 1: Detection & Reporting

Performed by: All Employees / SIEM / IT Help Desk

  1. Security incidents may be detected through:
    • Employee observation and reporting (phishing emails, suspicious behaviour, lost devices).
    • Automated SIEM alerts and correlation rules.
    • Endpoint detection and response (EDR) alerts.
    • Third-party notifications (vendor, CERT-In advisory, customer report).
    • Vulnerability scan findings indicating active exploitation.
  2. The reporter submits an incident report via email to [TO BE COMPLETED BY RAMDOOT SOLUTIONS] or the IT Help Desk portal, including: what was observed, when, affected system(s), and any actions already taken.
  3. The IT Help Desk logs the incident ticket and immediately escalates to the CSIRT on-call member.

Step 2: Triage & Classification

Performed by: CSIRT

  1. The CSIRT on-call member performs an initial assessment within 30 minutes of notification.
  2. Classify the incident by priority:
PriorityDescriptionExamplesResponse Time
P1 — CriticalActive breach, data exfiltration, ransomware, or complete service outage.Confirmed data breach, ransomware encryption in progress, production down.Immediate (within 15 min)
P2 — HighSignificant threat with potential for major impact; containment needed urgently.Compromised privileged account, malware spreading on network, targeted phishing campaign.Within 1 hour
P3 — MediumConfirmed incident with limited scope; no active data loss.Single endpoint malware, unauthorized access attempt blocked, policy violation.Within 4 hours
P4 — LowMinor event; informational or low-risk.Failed login attempts below threshold, phishing email reported but not clicked, false positive alert.Within 24 hours
  1. Assign an Incident Commander for P1 and P2 incidents.
  2. Activate the incident response communication channel for P1/P2.
  3. Begin the incident timeline log documenting all actions and findings with timestamps.

Step 3: Containment

Performed by: CSIRT / IT Operations

  1. Short-term containment (stop the bleeding):
    • Isolate affected systems from the network (disable network port, move to quarantine VLAN).
    • Block malicious IPs, domains, or email addresses at the firewall/proxy/email gateway.
    • Disable compromised user accounts.
    • Revoke compromised API keys, tokens, or certificates.
  2. Evidence preservation (before any destructive actions):
    • Capture memory dumps of affected systems.
    • Create forensic disk images before any remediation.
    • Preserve relevant log files (system, application, network, SIEM).
    • Document chain of custody for all evidence collected.
  3. Long-term containment (if systems must remain operational):
    • Apply temporary patches or configuration changes to prevent further exploitation.
    • Increase monitoring and logging on affected and adjacent systems.
    • Implement additional network segmentation if needed.

Step 4: Eradication

Performed by: CSIRT / IT Operations

  1. Identify and eliminate the root cause:
    • Remove malware, backdoors, and unauthorized accounts.
    • Patch the exploited vulnerability.
    • Reset credentials for all potentially compromised accounts.
    • Rebuild compromised systems from known-good images if integrity cannot be assured.
  2. Verify eradication through scanning and monitoring before proceeding to recovery.
  3. Update IoCs in security tools (SIEM rules, firewall blocklists, EDR signatures).

Step 5: Recovery

Performed by: IT Operations / CSIRT

  1. Restore affected systems to normal operation:
    • Restore from verified clean backups if systems were rebuilt.
    • Reconnect isolated systems to the network in a controlled manner.
    • Re-enable disabled accounts after credential reset and MFA verification.
  2. Implement enhanced monitoring for a minimum of 30 days post-recovery to detect any recurrence.
  3. Validate that all business functions are restored and operating normally.
  4. Communicate restoration status to affected stakeholders.

Step 6: Post-Incident Review

Performed by: CSIRT / Incident Commander

  1. Conduct a post-incident review (blameless post-mortem) within 5 business days of incident closure for P1/P2 incidents, 10 business days for P3.
  2. Review the complete incident timeline and document:
    • What happened (root cause, attack vector, timeline).
    • What went well in the response.
    • What could be improved.
    • Action items with owners and deadlines.
  3. Update the incident response procedure, runbooks, and detection rules based on lessons learned.
  4. Share sanitized lessons learned with the broader organization where appropriate.

Step 7: Reporting

Performed by: CSIRT / Legal / Compliance

  1. Internal reporting: Provide a written incident report to Management within 5 business days of closure.
  2. CERT-In notification (mandatory for qualifying cyber incidents):
    • Report to CERT-In within 6 hours of becoming aware of the incident, as per CERT-In Directions dated 28 April 2022.
    • Qualifying incidents include: targeted scanning/probing, compromise of systems, data breaches, ransomware attacks, attacks on critical infrastructure, and identity theft/phishing attacks.
    • Use CERT-In's designated reporting portal or email ([TO BE COMPLETED BY RAMDOOT SOLUTIONS]).
    • Provide follow-up reports as required by CERT-In.
  3. Data breach notification: If personal data is compromised, notify affected data subjects and the relevant Data Protection Authority as required by applicable law.
  4. Customer notification: If client data is affected, notify impacted clients per contractual SLA and regulatory requirements. Legal must review all external communications before release.

7. Verification / Completion Criteria

  • The incident is fully contained, root cause identified, and affected systems restored.
  • All evidence is preserved and chain of custody documented.
  • CERT-In notification submitted within 6 hours (for qualifying incidents).
  • Post-incident review completed with action items assigned.
  • Incident report filed and distributed to relevant stakeholders.
  • Detection rules and response runbooks updated with lessons learned.

9. Exceptions

Exceptions must be approved by [TO BE COMPLETED BY RAMDOOT SOLUTIONS].

  • Simulated Incidents: Planned red-team exercises and tabletop drills follow a pre-approved test plan and do not trigger CERT-In reporting. They must be clearly marked as exercises in all communications.
  • False Positives: Events confirmed as false positives after initial triage may be closed with a brief justification note. They do not require full incident response lifecycle execution but must still be logged.
