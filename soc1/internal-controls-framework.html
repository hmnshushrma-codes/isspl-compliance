1. Framework Overview

This document establishes Ramdoot Solutions' internal controls framework for controls relevant to customer entities' Internal Control over Financial Reporting (ICFR). The framework serves as the foundational reference for designing, implementing, operating, and monitoring internal controls that may be subject to a SOC 1 examination.

1.1 Objectives

  • Provide reasonable assurance that controls relevant to customer ICFR are suitably designed and operating effectively;
  • Align Ramdoot Solutions' control environment with the COSO 2013 Internal Control — Integrated Framework;
  • Establish clear ownership, governance, and accountability for internal controls;
  • Support management's ability to prepare a description of the service organisation's system and assert on the design and effectiveness of controls.

1.2 Framework Scope

This framework covers controls related to services provided by Ramdoot Solutions that may affect customer financial reporting. The specific services in scope are subject to the applicability assessment documented in RS-SOC1-001, Section 4.

Applicability Notice: SOC 1 applicability requires confirmation based on the services provided to customers and their relationship to Internal Control over Financial Reporting (ICFR). This framework should be tailored once applicability is formally determined.

1.3 Guiding Principles

  • Risk-Based: Controls are prioritised based on the risk they mitigate to customer financial reporting.
  • Proportionate: The depth and formality of controls are proportionate to the significance of the service and the associated risk.
  • Documented: All controls are formally documented with owners, procedures, frequencies, and evidence requirements.
  • Testable: Controls produce evidence that can be independently tested by an auditor.
  • Continuously Improved: The framework is subject to periodic review and improvement.

2. COSO Framework Alignment

The Committee of Sponsoring Organizations of the Treadway Commission (COSO) published the Internal Control — Integrated Framework in 2013, which is the most widely accepted framework for internal controls over financial reporting. Ramdoot Solutions adopts this framework as the basis for its internal controls.

2.1 COSO Components

COSO defines five interrelated components of internal control, supported by 17 principles:

ComponentDescriptionCOSO Principles
Control Environment The set of standards, processes, and structures that provide the basis for carrying out internal control across the organisation. Principles 1 – 5
Risk Assessment The process for identifying and analysing risks to the achievement of objectives, forming a basis for determining how risks should be managed. Principles 6 – 9
Control Activities The actions established through policies and procedures that help ensure management directives to mitigate risks are carried out. Principles 10 – 12
Information & Communication The information necessary for the organisation to carry out internal control responsibilities, and the communication of that information internally and externally. Principles 13 – 15
Monitoring Activities Ongoing evaluations, separate evaluations, or a combination thereof to ascertain whether each of the five components of internal control is present and functioning. Principles 16 – 17

3.1 Commitment to Integrity and Ethical Values (COSO Principle 1)

  • Tone at the Top: Senior management sets expectations for ethical behaviour through communication and by example.
  • Compliance Reporting: A mechanism exists for personnel to report ethical concerns or violations without fear of retaliation.

4. Risk Assessment for Financial Controls

Risk assessment is the process of identifying and evaluating risks that may prevent the organisation from achieving its control objectives related to customer ICFR.

4.1 Risk Identification (COSO Principles 6–7)

Risks to the achievement of SOC 1 control objectives are identified through:

  • Analysis of services provided to customers and their relationship to financial reporting;
  • Mapping of data flows through in-scope systems, identifying points where financial data could be compromised;
  • Review of past incidents, audit findings, and industry threat intelligence;
  • Interviews with process owners and key personnel.

Risk Categories

CategoryDescriptionExamples
Logical Access Risks related to unauthorised access to systems or data Credential compromise, excessive privileges, stale accounts
Processing Integrity Risks related to inaccurate, incomplete, or untimely data processing Data corruption, calculation errors, missed processing windows
Change Management Risks related to unauthorised or untested changes to in-scope systems Unauthorised code deployment, missing test coverage, regression
Availability Risks related to service disruptions affecting financial processing System outages, data loss, infrastructure failure
Vendor / Subservice Risks related to reliance on third parties for in-scope services Vendor security breach, service degradation, contract non-compliance
People Risks related to personnel competence, integrity, or availability Key person dependency, insider threat, training gaps

4.2 Risk Analysis

Identified risks are analysed using the following dimensions:

DimensionScaleDescription
Likelihood Low / Medium / High The probability that the risk will materialise within the reporting period.
Impact Low / Medium / High / Critical The potential effect on customer financial reporting if the risk materialises.
Inherent Risk Low / Medium / High / Critical Risk level before considering mitigating controls.
Residual Risk Low / Medium / High / Critical Risk level after considering mitigating controls.

4.3 Fraud Risk Assessment (COSO Principle 8)

The organisation considers the potential for fraud in assessing risks to the achievement of objectives. Fraud risk assessment for SOC 1-relevant controls includes:

  • Fraudulent Financial Reporting: Risk that financial data processed on behalf of customers could be intentionally manipulated.
  • Misappropriation of Assets: Risk that customer data or assets could be misappropriated by personnel with access to in-scope systems.
  • Management Override: Risk that management could override controls to manipulate processing or data.
  • Incentives and Pressures: Assessment of factors that could create incentives for fraudulent activity.

4.4 Change-Driven Risk (COSO Principle 9)

The organisation identifies and assesses changes that could significantly impact the system of internal control:

  • Changes to the external environment (regulatory, industry, economic);
  • Changes to the business model or services provided;
  • Changes to leadership or key personnel;
  • Changes to systems or technology;
  • Rapid growth or expansion of services.

When significant changes are identified, risk assessments are updated and control activities are revised as necessary.

5. Control Activities

Control activities are the actions established through policies and procedures that help ensure management directives to mitigate risks to the achievement of control objectives are carried out.

5.1 Types of Control Activities (COSO Principle 10)

Ramdoot Solutions employs the following types of control activities:

TypeDescriptionExamples
Preventive Controls that prevent errors or irregularities from occurring in the first place. Access restrictions, input validation, segregation of duties, approval workflows
Detective Controls that detect errors or irregularities after they have occurred. Access reviews, reconciliations, log monitoring, exception reports
Corrective Controls that correct errors or irregularities once detected. Incident response, rollback procedures, data correction processes

The specific control activities mapped to each control objective are detailed in the SOC 1 Control Matrix (RS-SOC1-003).

5.2 IT General Controls (COSO Principle 11)

IT General Controls (ITGCs) are controls over the technology environment that support the reliable operation of application controls and data integrity. ITGCs relevant to SOC 1 include:

5.2.1 Logical Access Controls

  • User access provisioning and de-provisioning based on role and need;
  • Periodic access reviews to ensure access remains appropriate;
  • Authentication controls (password policies, MFA for privileged access);
  • Privileged access management and monitoring;
  • Network segmentation and firewall controls.

5.2.2 Change Management

  • Formal change request, review, and approval process;
  • Testing in non-production environments before deployment;
  • Segregation of development, testing, and production environments;
  • Version control and code review;
  • Rollback procedures for failed changes.

5.2.3 Computer Operations

  • Data backup and recovery procedures;
  • Job scheduling and monitoring;
  • System and database administration procedures;
  • Capacity monitoring and planning;
  • Disaster recovery planning and testing.

5.3 Application Controls (COSO Principle 12 — Part 1)

Application controls are specific to individual applications and ensure the completeness, accuracy, and validity of data processing. These include:

  • Input Controls: Validation of data at the point of entry (format checks, range checks, completeness checks);
  • Processing Controls: Automated checks during processing to ensure accuracy and completeness (batch totals, control totals, exception handling);
  • Output Controls: Verification that output is complete, accurate, and delivered to the correct recipients;
  • Interface Controls: Controls over data transfers between systems to ensure integrity during transmission.

6. Information & Communication

The organisation obtains or generates and uses relevant, quality information to support the functioning of internal control.

6.1 Internal Communication (COSO Principle 14)

The organisation internally communicates information, including objectives and responsibilities for internal control, necessary to support the functioning of internal control:

  • Escalation Channels: Clear escalation paths exist for reporting control deficiencies, incidents, or concerns.
  • Management Reporting: Regular reports on control effectiveness, incidents, and deficiencies are provided to management.

7. Monitoring Activities

Monitoring ensures that internal controls continue to operate effectively over time. It consists of ongoing monitoring activities, separate evaluations, and deficiency reporting.

7.1 Ongoing Monitoring (COSO Principle 16 — Part 1)

Ongoing monitoring activities are built into the normal course of operations and include:

  • Automated Monitoring: System-generated alerts for access violations, processing errors, failed jobs, and security events.
  • Management Reviews: Regular management reviews of control performance indicators, dashboards, and exception reports.
  • Supervisory Activities: Supervisors verify that control activities are performed as designed during day-to-day operations.
  • Self-Assessment: Control owners periodically self-assess the effectiveness of their controls.

7.3 Deficiency Reporting (COSO Principle 17)

The organisation evaluates and communicates internal control deficiencies in a timely manner to those parties responsible for taking corrective action, including senior management and the governing body:

Deficiency Classification

ClassificationDefinitionEscalation
Critical A control deficiency or combination of deficiencies that results in a reasonable possibility that a material misstatement in customer financial reporting will not be prevented or detected. Equivalent to a material weakness. Immediate escalation to senior management and governing body
High A control deficiency or combination of deficiencies that is less severe than a material weakness yet important enough to merit attention by those responsible for oversight. Equivalent to a significant deficiency. Escalation to senior management within 5 business days
Medium A control deficiency that does not rise to the level of a significant deficiency but represents an area for improvement. Reported to control owner and management at next review
Low A minor observation or opportunity for improvement that does not represent a control deficiency. Tracked by control owner for continuous improvement

Deficiency Tracking: All identified deficiencies are tracked in a centralised register with assigned owners, remediation plans, and target dates. Status is reviewed by management at least quarterly.
