1. Governance & Objectives

1.1 Purpose

This Network & Information Security Policy (“NISP” or “Policy”) establishes the overarching security governance framework for Ramdoot Solutions (“the Organization”). It defines management direction, principles, and mandatory controls to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information assets owned, processed, or managed by the Organization.

1.2 Scope

This Policy shall apply to all employees, contractors, consultants, temporary workers, and third-party personnel who access, process, store, or transmit Ramdoot Solutions' information assets. It covers all technology environments including on-premises infrastructure, cloud services, remote working environments, mobile devices, and third-party hosted systems.

1.3 Security Objectives

The Organization shall pursue the following security objectives:

  • Protect information assets against unauthorized access, disclosure, modification, destruction, or interference.
  • Ensure compliance with applicable Indian laws, regulatory requirements, and contractual obligations.
  • Maintain a risk-based approach to information security aligned with the Organization's business objectives.
  • Promote a culture of security awareness across all levels of the Organization.
  • Establish and maintain incident response capabilities to detect, contain, and recover from security events.
  • Enable business continuity through resilient infrastructure and documented recovery procedures.
  • Continuously improve the information security management framework through periodic review and audit.

1.4 Security Governance Structure

The Organization shall establish and maintain a security governance structure comprising the following bodies:

  • Management / Board: Shall provide strategic direction, approve this Policy, allocate resources, and bear ultimate accountability for information security.
  • Information Security Officer (ISO) / CISO: [TO BE COMPLETED BY RAMDOOT SOLUTIONS] shall be designated as the Information Security Officer. This individual shall be responsible for the day-to-day management of the security program, policy enforcement, risk assessment coordination, and regulatory compliance monitoring.
  • Information Security Committee: A cross-functional committee comprising representatives from Engineering, Operations, HR, Legal, and Management shall convene at least quarterly to review security posture, risk register, incidents, and policy effectiveness.
  • Department Heads: Shall be accountable for implementing security controls within their departments and ensuring staff compliance with this Policy.

1.5 Policy Authority

This Policy is approved by the Management of Ramdoot Solutions and shall take precedence over any conflicting departmental procedure or guideline. All subsidiary policies, standards, procedures, and guidelines must be consistent with the principles and requirements stated herein.

Alignment Note: This Policy is aligned with the SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria (Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, Privacy) and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls. The Organization does not claim ISO 27001 certification unless explicitly stated in a separate certification document.

3. Risk Management

3.1 Risk Assessment Methodology

The Organization shall adopt a structured risk assessment methodology to identify, analyze, evaluate, and treat information security risks. Risk assessments shall be conducted:

  • At least annually as part of the security program review cycle.
  • Prior to the introduction of significant new systems, services, or business processes.
  • Following a significant security incident or material change in the threat landscape.
  • When required by contractual or regulatory obligations.

3.2 Risk Identification & Analysis

Each risk shall be evaluated based on the following dimensions:

  • Likelihood: The probability of the threat exploiting a vulnerability (rated 1–5: Rare, Unlikely, Possible, Likely, Almost Certain).
  • Impact: The potential consequence to the Organization's operations, finances, reputation, or legal standing (rated 1–5: Negligible, Minor, Moderate, Major, Catastrophic).
  • Risk Score: Likelihood × Impact, yielding a score from 1 to 25.
Risk LevelScore RangeTreatment Requirement
Critical20 – 25Immediate action required; must be escalated to Management within 24 hours
High12 – 19Treatment plan required within 7 business days; ISO approval required
Medium6 – 11Treatment plan required within 30 business days
Low1 – 5Accept or monitor; document acceptance rationale

3.3 Risk Appetite

The Organization's risk appetite is defined as follows: Ramdoot Solutions shall not accept any Critical or High residual risk without explicit, documented approval from Management. Medium risks may be accepted by the ISO with documented justification. Low risks may be accepted by the relevant Asset Owner.

3.4 Risk Treatment Options

For each identified risk, one of the following treatment options shall be selected:

  • Mitigate: Implement controls to reduce likelihood and/or impact to an acceptable level.
  • Transfer: Transfer the risk to a third party (e.g., insurance, outsourcing with SLAs).
  • Avoid: Eliminate the risk by discontinuing the activity or removing the asset.
  • Accept: Acknowledge and accept the residual risk with documented approval at the appropriate authority level.

3.5 Risk Register

The ISO shall maintain a Risk Register documenting all identified risks, their current scores, treatment plans, responsible owners, and review dates. The Risk Register shall be reviewed by the Information Security Committee at least quarterly.

4. Asset Management

4.1 Asset Inventory

The Organization shall maintain a comprehensive inventory of all information assets including hardware, software, data repositories, network components, cloud services, and intellectual property. The inventory shall be updated at least quarterly and upon any significant change.

4.2 Asset Classification

All information assets shall be classified according to the data classification scheme defined in Section 5 of this Policy. Asset classification shall determine the minimum security controls applied to the asset.

4.3 Asset Ownership

Every information asset shall have a designated Asset Owner who is responsible for:

  • Ensuring the asset is properly classified and the classification is reviewed periodically.
  • Authorizing access to the asset based on business need and least privilege principles.
  • Ensuring appropriate security controls are applied commensurate with the asset's classification.
  • Participating in risk assessments related to the asset.
  • Approving the decommissioning and secure disposal of the asset at end of life.

4.4 Asset Lifecycle Management

Assets shall be managed throughout their lifecycle: procurement, deployment, operation, maintenance, and disposal. At each stage:

  • Procurement: Security requirements shall be evaluated before acquisition. Vendor security assessments shall be performed for third-party software and services.
  • Deployment: Assets shall be hardened according to approved baseline configurations before being placed into production.
  • Operation: Assets shall be patched, monitored, and subject to periodic security review.
  • Disposal: Assets shall be decommissioned and disposed of securely in accordance with Section 31 (Data Retention & Disposal).

4.5 Removable Media

The use of removable media (USB drives, external hard drives, optical discs) is prohibited unless explicitly authorized by the ISO. Where authorized, all data on removable media must be encrypted using approved cryptographic methods (see Section 16). Removable media containing Confidential or Restricted data shall be tracked in the asset inventory and securely destroyed when no longer required.

6. Acceptable Use

6.1 General Principles

All IT resources provided by the Organization (including computers, networks, email, internet access, cloud services, and software) are the property of Ramdoot Solutions and are provided for authorized business purposes. Limited personal use is permitted provided it does not interfere with work duties, consume excessive resources, or violate any provision of this Policy.

6.2 Acceptable Use

Personnel are authorized to use IT resources for:

  • Business-related activities within the scope of their role.
  • Authorized research, training, and professional development.
  • Brief, incidental personal use that does not violate any other policy provision.

6.3 Prohibited Activities

The following activities are strictly prohibited:

  • Accessing, downloading, storing, or distributing illegal, obscene, offensive, or discriminatory material.
  • Unauthorized access to systems, applications, or data not required for the individual's role.
  • Sharing credentials, authentication tokens, or access keys with any other person.
  • Installing unauthorized software, browser extensions, or hardware on Organization-owned devices.
  • Circumventing or attempting to circumvent security controls, firewalls, content filters, or access restrictions.
  • Using Organization resources for personal commercial activities, political campaigning, or cryptocurrency mining.
  • Sending unsolicited bulk communications (spam) from Organization systems.
  • Connecting unauthorized devices to the corporate network without IT Operations approval.
  • Storing Restricted or Confidential data on personal devices or unapproved cloud services.
  • Disabling or tampering with endpoint security software (antivirus, EDR, firewall).

6.4 Internet & Email Use

Internet and email usage shall be monitored by the Organization for security purposes. Personnel shall not have an expectation of privacy when using Organization-provided communication systems. All email communications must comply with the Organization's data classification rules. Auto-forwarding of Organization email to personal accounts is prohibited.

6.5 Personal Devices

Personal devices used to access Organization data or systems must comply with Section 37 (Mobile Devices) and be enrolled in the Organization's Mobile Device Management (MDM) solution. The Organization reserves the right to remotely wipe Organization data from personal devices upon employee separation or device loss.

7. Identity & Access Management

7.1 Principles

Access to all information systems shall be governed by the following principles:

  • Least Privilege: Users shall be granted the minimum level of access necessary to perform their job functions.
  • Need-to-Know: Access to information shall be restricted to individuals who require it for legitimate business purposes.
  • Separation of Duties: Critical functions shall be divided among different individuals to prevent fraud or error.
  • Default Deny: Access shall be denied by default; all access must be explicitly granted through an approved process.

7.2 Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

The Organization shall implement role-based access control for all production systems and applications. Access roles shall be defined based on job functions and approved by the relevant Asset Owner and department manager. Role definitions shall be reviewed at least semi-annually.

7.3 Access Provisioning

Access shall be provisioned only upon receipt of a formal, documented access request that includes:

  • Identification of the requesting individual and their role.
  • Specific systems, applications, and access levels requested.
  • Business justification for the access.
  • Approval from the individual's manager and the Asset Owner (for Confidential/Restricted resources).

7.4 Access Reviews

Access rights shall be reviewed at least quarterly for all systems containing Confidential or Restricted data, and at least semi-annually for all other systems. Access reviews shall verify that each user's access remains appropriate for their current role and that no unauthorized accounts exist.

7.5 Access Deprovisioning

Access shall be revoked immediately (within 4 business hours) upon termination of employment or contract. Access shall be modified within 2 business days upon role change or transfer. See Section 34 (Employee Offboarding) for detailed procedures.

7.6 Service Accounts

Service accounts shall be inventoried, assigned an owner, and subject to the same access review requirements as user accounts. Service account credentials shall be stored in an approved secrets management solution and rotated at least every 90 days.

8. Privileged Access

8.1 Scope

Privileged access includes administrator accounts, root accounts, database administrator accounts, cloud management console access, and any access that grants the ability to modify system configurations, security controls, or access controls.

8.2 Authorization

Privileged access shall require separate, documented authorization from the ISO and the individual's line manager. Privileged accounts shall be separate from standard user accounts; administrators must use their standard account for day-to-day activities and elevate to the privileged account only when performing administrative tasks.

8.3 Controls

  • All privileged access sessions shall be logged and monitored. Logs shall be protected from tampering and retained for a minimum of 12 months.
  • Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is mandatory for all privileged access.
  • Privileged access shall be time-limited where technically feasible. Just-in-time (JIT) access provisioning shall be implemented for production environments.
  • The number of privileged accounts shall be minimized and reviewed monthly by the ISO.
  • Privileged account credentials shall be stored in an approved privileged access management (PAM) solution or secrets vault.
  • Shared privileged accounts are prohibited unless a documented exception is approved by the ISO. Where shared accounts exist, all individual actions must be attributable through session logging.

8.4 Emergency Access

Break-glass (emergency) access procedures shall be documented and tested annually. Emergency access shall require post-access review within 24 hours. All actions performed under emergency access shall be fully audited.

9.2 Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA shall be required for the following:

  • All access to production systems and environments.
  • All privileged / administrative access.
  • Remote access to the corporate network (VPN).
  • Cloud management consoles (AWS, Azure, GCP, etc.).
  • Email and collaboration platforms accessed from outside the corporate network.
  • Access to any system containing Restricted or Confidential data.

Acceptable MFA factors include TOTP authenticator applications, hardware security keys (FIDO2/WebAuthn), and push notifications from approved authenticator apps. SMS-based OTP is discouraged and shall be used only as a fallback where no other option is available.

9.3 Single Sign-On (SSO)

The Organization shall implement SSO where technically feasible to reduce password fatigue and centralize authentication control. All SSO-integrated applications shall enforce the MFA requirements specified above.

9.4 Credential Storage

Passwords shall never be stored in plaintext. Application systems shall use approved hashing algorithms (bcrypt, scrypt, or Argon2id) with appropriate salt and work factor. API keys, tokens, and secrets shall be stored in an approved secrets management solution and shall not be hardcoded in source code or configuration files.

10. Network Security

10.1 Network Architecture

The Organization's network shall be designed with defense-in-depth principles. Network segmentation shall be implemented to isolate environments with different security requirements (e.g., production, development, staging, guest, management).

10.2 Firewalls

  • Firewalls shall be deployed at all network perimeter points and between security zones.
  • Firewall rules shall follow a default-deny policy: all traffic is blocked unless explicitly permitted.
  • Firewall rules shall be reviewed at least quarterly and after any significant network change.
  • Unused or obsolete rules shall be removed promptly.
  • All firewall configuration changes shall be documented, approved by the IT Operations Lead, and logged.

10.3 Network Segmentation

The following network zones shall be maintained:

  • DMZ: Public-facing services shall be deployed in a demilitarized zone separated from internal networks.
  • Production Zone: Production servers and databases shall be in a restricted network segment accessible only through controlled entry points.
  • Development/Staging Zone: Development and staging environments shall be segregated from production. Production data shall not be used in non-production environments without anonymization.
  • Management Zone: Administrative interfaces and management tools shall be accessible only from a dedicated management network.
  • Guest Network: A separate, isolated network shall be provided for guest and visitor devices with no access to internal resources.

10.4 VPN

Remote access to internal network resources shall be permitted only through an Organization-approved VPN solution. VPN connections shall use strong encryption (AES-256 or equivalent), enforce MFA, and be logged. Split tunneling is prohibited for VPN connections accessing Confidential or Restricted resources.

10.5 Wireless Security

  • Corporate wireless networks shall use WPA3 (or WPA2-Enterprise as minimum) with 802.1X authentication.
  • Unauthorized wireless access points are prohibited and shall be detected through periodic wireless scanning.
  • Guest wireless shall be isolated and shall not provide access to internal network resources.

10.6 Intrusion Detection / Prevention

Network intrusion detection/prevention systems (IDS/IPS) shall be deployed at network perimeter points and within critical network segments. Signatures and rulesets shall be updated at least daily. Alerts shall be routed to the security monitoring team for investigation.

11. Endpoint Security

11.1 Antivirus & Endpoint Detection and Response (EDR)

All Organization-owned endpoints (desktops, laptops, servers) shall have approved antivirus and/or EDR software installed and actively running. Endpoint protection software shall not be disabled, paused, or uninstalled by end users. Signature databases shall be updated automatically at least daily.

11.2 Endpoint Hardening

All endpoints shall be configured according to approved hardening baselines that include:

  • Disabling unnecessary services, protocols, and ports.
  • Removing or disabling default accounts and changing default passwords.
  • Enabling host-based firewalls.
  • Enabling full-disk encryption (BitLocker on Windows, FileVault on macOS, LUKS on Linux).
  • Enabling automatic screen lock after a maximum of 10 minutes of inactivity.
  • Restricting local administrator privileges to authorized personnel only.

11.3 Patch Management (Endpoints)

All endpoints shall receive operating system and application patches in accordance with Section 20 (Patch Management). Endpoints that are not compliant with patching requirements within the defined timelines shall be quarantined from the network until remediated.

11.4 Software Inventory

Only authorized software shall be installed on Organization endpoints. An approved software list shall be maintained by IT Operations. Unauthorized software shall be detected and removed. Application whitelisting shall be implemented where technically feasible for critical systems.

The Organization shall maintain a list of approved cloud service providers. Only approved providers shall be used for hosting Organization data or services. Approval requires a vendor risk assessment (see Section 29).

12.2 Shared Responsibility Model

The Organization shall document and understand the shared responsibility model for each cloud service used, clearly delineating the security responsibilities of the cloud provider and the Organization. The ISO shall ensure that the Organization fulfills all customer-side security responsibilities.

12.3 Cloud Configuration Requirements

  • All cloud resources shall be deployed using infrastructure-as-code (IaC) or documented, repeatable processes.
  • Cloud storage buckets and objects shall not be publicly accessible unless explicitly required and approved.
  • Cloud security groups and access control lists shall follow the principle of least privilege.
  • Cloud management console access shall require MFA and shall be restricted to authorized personnel.
  • Cloud configuration shall be continuously monitored using cloud security posture management (CSPM) tools or equivalent periodic review.
  • Data residency requirements shall be enforced; Organization data shall not be stored in jurisdictions that conflict with applicable Indian regulations without documented legal review.

12.4 Shadow IT

The use of unapproved cloud services (“shadow IT”) for processing or storing Organization data is prohibited. Personnel shall report any discovered use of unapproved cloud services to the ISO.

13. Application Security

13.1 Secure Development Principles

All applications developed by or for the Organization shall adhere to secure development principles throughout the software development lifecycle. Security shall be integrated into every phase: requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment, and maintenance.

13.2 OWASP Top 10

Development teams shall be trained on and shall implement controls to mitigate the current OWASP Top 10 risks. At minimum, applications must address:

  • Broken Access Control: Implement server-side access control enforcement; deny by default.
  • Cryptographic Failures: Encrypt sensitive data in transit and at rest; do not use deprecated algorithms.
  • Injection: Use parameterized queries, prepared statements, and ORM frameworks; validate all input.
  • Insecure Design: Conduct threat modeling during design phase; implement defense-in-depth.
  • Security Misconfiguration: Harden all environments; remove default credentials and unnecessary features.
  • Vulnerable and Outdated Components: Maintain dependency inventories; patch promptly.
  • Identification and Authentication Failures: Implement strong authentication; protect against credential stuffing.
  • Software and Data Integrity Failures: Verify integrity of updates and CI/CD pipelines.
  • Security Logging and Monitoring Failures: Log security-relevant events; ensure alerts are actionable.
  • Server-Side Request Forgery (SSRF): Validate and sanitize all URLs; restrict outbound network access.

13.3 Input Validation & Output Encoding

All user-supplied input shall be validated on the server side against expected formats, lengths, and data types. All output rendered to users shall be encoded appropriately for the output context (HTML, JavaScript, URL, CSS) to prevent cross-site scripting (XSS) attacks.

13.4 API Security

All APIs shall implement authentication, authorization, rate limiting, input validation, and logging. API keys and tokens shall be transmitted only over encrypted channels (TLS 1.2+). API documentation shall not be publicly accessible for internal APIs.

14. Secure Development Lifecycle

14.1 Requirements Phase

Security requirements shall be defined alongside functional requirements for every project. Threat modeling shall be performed for all projects involving new external-facing functionality or changes to authentication, authorization, or data handling.

14.2 Code Review

All code changes shall undergo peer review by at least one other developer before being merged to the main branch. Security-critical code (authentication, authorization, cryptography, input handling) shall require review by a designated security-aware reviewer.

14.3 Static Application Security Testing (SAST)

Automated SAST tools shall be integrated into the CI/CD pipeline. All code shall be scanned before deployment to production. Critical and High severity findings shall block deployment until resolved. Medium findings shall be tracked and resolved within the next sprint.

14.4 Dynamic Application Security Testing (DAST)

DAST scans shall be performed against staging environments at least monthly and before major releases. Findings shall be triaged, prioritized, and remediated according to the vulnerability management SLAs in Section 19.

14.5 Dependency Scanning

Software Composition Analysis (SCA) tools shall be used to identify known vulnerabilities in third-party libraries and dependencies. Dependency scans shall run automatically in the CI/CD pipeline. Dependencies with known Critical or High vulnerabilities shall be updated or replaced before production deployment.

14.6 Secrets Detection

Automated secrets detection tools shall be integrated into the CI/CD pipeline and pre-commit hooks to prevent accidental commits of credentials, API keys, tokens, or certificates. Any detected secret shall trigger an immediate pipeline failure and the compromised credential shall be rotated.

14.7 Environment Separation

Development, staging, and production environments shall be logically separated. Production credentials and data shall not be used in development or staging environments. Deployment to production shall follow a controlled, documented release process.

15. Source Code Security

15.1 Repository Access

Source code repositories shall be hosted on an approved platform ([TO BE COMPLETED BY RAMDOOT SOLUTIONS]). Access to repositories shall be granted on a need-to-know basis using role-based permissions. All repository access shall require authentication via SSO and MFA.

15.2 Branch Protection

  • The main (or master) branch shall be protected: direct pushes are prohibited.
  • All changes to protected branches shall require a pull request with at least one approved review.
  • CI checks (build, tests, SAST, secrets detection) shall pass before merge is permitted.
  • Force-pushes to protected branches are prohibited.
  • Branch protection rules shall not be bypassed without ISO approval and documented justification.

15.3 Secrets Management

Source code and configuration files shall not contain hardcoded secrets, passwords, API keys, or encryption keys. Secrets shall be managed through an approved secrets management solution (e.g., HashiCorp Vault, AWS Secrets Manager, environment-specific secret stores). Pre-commit hooks shall scan for potential secrets.

15.4 Third-Party Code

Third-party libraries and open-source components shall be evaluated for security, license compliance, and maintenance status before inclusion. A software bill of materials (SBOM) shall be maintained for all production applications.

16.2 Prohibited Algorithms

The following algorithms are prohibited for any use: DES, 3DES, RC4, MD5 (for security purposes), SHA-1 (for security purposes), RSA with key length less than 2048 bits.

16.3 Key Management

  • Cryptographic keys shall be generated using cryptographically secure random number generators.
  • Key generation, storage, distribution, rotation, and destruction shall be documented and controlled.
  • Private keys shall be stored in hardware security modules (HSMs), approved key management services, or encrypted key stores with restricted access.
  • Symmetric encryption keys shall be rotated at least annually, or upon suspected compromise.
  • Key destruction shall use approved methods that prevent recovery.

16.4 Certificate Management

TLS/SSL certificates shall be obtained from trusted Certificate Authorities. Certificate expiration shall be monitored, and renewal shall be initiated at least 30 days before expiry. Automated certificate management (e.g., ACME/Let's Encrypt with auto-renewal) is encouraged. Revoked or expired certificates shall be replaced immediately.

17. Encryption in Transit

17.1 TLS Requirements

  • All data transmitted over public or untrusted networks shall be encrypted using TLS 1.2 or TLS 1.3.
  • TLS 1.0 and TLS 1.1 are prohibited and shall be disabled on all systems.
  • SSL (all versions) is prohibited.
  • HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS) shall be enabled on all web applications with a minimum max-age of 31536000 seconds (1 year) and the includeSubDomains directive.

17.2 Cipher Suites

Only cipher suites providing forward secrecy shall be used. Approved cipher suites include:

  • TLS_AES_256_GCM_SHA384 (TLS 1.3)
  • TLS_AES_128_GCM_SHA256 (TLS 1.3)
  • TLS_CHACHA20_POLY1305_SHA256 (TLS 1.3)
  • TLS_ECDHE_RSA_WITH_AES_256_GCM_SHA384 (TLS 1.2)
  • TLS_ECDHE_RSA_WITH_AES_128_GCM_SHA256 (TLS 1.2)
  • TLS_ECDHE_ECDSA_WITH_AES_256_GCM_SHA384 (TLS 1.2)

Cipher suites using NULL encryption, RC4, DES, 3DES, or export-grade cryptography are prohibited.

17.3 Internal Communications

Encryption in transit is required for all internal service-to-service communication that traverses network segment boundaries. Mutual TLS (mTLS) is recommended for service-to-service communication in production environments.

17.4 Email Encryption

Emails containing Restricted or Confidential data shall be encrypted using S/MIME, PGP, or the Organization's approved email encryption solution. Where end-to-end email encryption is not feasible, such data shall be transmitted as an encrypted attachment with the decryption key shared via a separate channel.

18. Encryption at Rest

18.1 Database Encryption

All databases containing Confidential or Restricted data shall implement encryption at rest using AES-256 or equivalent. Transparent Data Encryption (TDE) or full-disk encryption of the underlying storage is required at minimum. Column-level encryption shall be applied to highly sensitive fields (e.g., personal identification numbers, financial data, health data).

18.2 File & Object Storage Encryption

All files and objects stored on servers, network-attached storage (NAS), or cloud storage shall be encrypted at rest. Server-side encryption with customer-managed keys (SSE-CMK) is preferred for cloud storage. Encryption keys shall be managed in accordance with Section 16.3.

18.3 Backup Encryption

All backup media (tapes, disks, cloud backups) shall be encrypted using AES-256. Backup encryption keys shall be stored separately from the backup media and managed through the Organization's key management procedures. Unencrypted backups are prohibited.

18.4 Endpoint Encryption

Full-disk encryption shall be enabled on all Organization-owned laptops, desktops, and mobile devices. Recovery keys shall be escrowed centrally and accessible only by IT Operations and the ISO.

20.2 Patch Management Process

  1. Identification: IT Operations shall monitor vendor advisories, CVE databases, and security mailing lists for applicable patches.
  2. Assessment: Each patch shall be assessed for applicability, severity, and potential impact on business operations.
  3. Testing: Patches shall be tested in a non-production environment before deployment to production (except for critical zero-day patches where the risk of delay exceeds the risk of untested deployment).
  4. Deployment: Approved patches shall be deployed to production within the timelines specified above.
  5. Verification: Post-deployment verification shall confirm the patch was successfully applied and no adverse effects occurred.
  6. Documentation: All patching activities shall be documented, including patch identity, affected systems, deployment date, and verification results.

20.3 Unsupported Software

Software that is no longer supported by the vendor (end-of-life) shall be replaced or upgraded within 90 days of end-of-support. Where immediate replacement is not feasible, a documented risk exception shall be obtained per Section 39 (Exceptions), and compensating controls shall be implemented.

21. Malware Protection

21.1 Antivirus / Anti-Malware

All endpoints and servers shall have approved anti-malware software installed and enabled. Real-time scanning shall be active at all times. Signature updates shall be applied automatically at least daily. Full system scans shall be performed at least weekly.

21.2 Email Filtering

All inbound and outbound email shall pass through the Organization's email security gateway, which shall provide: spam filtering, malware attachment scanning, URL rewriting and sandboxing, and protection against phishing and business email compromise (BEC) attacks. Executable attachments shall be blocked by default.

21.3 Web Filtering

Web traffic shall be filtered to block access to known malicious domains, command-and-control servers, and categories of websites that pose a security risk (e.g., malware distribution, phishing). DNS-level filtering shall be applied across all network segments.

21.4 Removable Media Scanning

Where removable media use is authorized (see Section 4.5), all removable media shall be automatically scanned for malware upon connection to any Organization endpoint.

24. Incident Response

24.1 Incident Classification

SeverityDefinitionExamples
SEV-1 (Critical)Active breach, data exfiltration, complete service outage affecting clients, or regulatory-reportable eventRansomware attack, confirmed data breach, complete infrastructure compromise
SEV-2 (High)Significant security event with potential for data exposure or major service degradationUnauthorized access to production systems, malware spread across endpoints, partial service outage
SEV-3 (Medium)Security event requiring investigation with limited immediate impactPhishing email delivered, unauthorized software installation, policy violation
SEV-4 (Low)Minor security event with negligible impactBlocked malware detection, failed brute-force attempt, minor misconfiguration

24.2 Response Phases

  1. Detection & Identification: Detect the incident through monitoring, alerts, or reports. Assign initial severity. Notify the Incident Response Coordinator.
  2. Containment: Implement immediate measures to limit the scope and impact of the incident. Short-term containment (isolate affected systems) followed by long-term containment (apply temporary fixes while maintaining evidence).
  3. Eradication: Remove the root cause of the incident (malware, compromised accounts, vulnerable components). Verify eradication through scanning and review.
  4. Recovery: Restore affected systems to normal operation from clean backups or verified clean state. Monitor for signs of recurring compromise.
  5. Post-Incident Review: Conduct a post-mortem within 5 business days of incident closure. Document lessons learned, root cause, timeline, impact, and corrective actions. Update controls and procedures as needed.

24.3 Communication

  • Internal communication during active incidents shall use a designated secure channel.
  • External communication (clients, regulators, media) shall be coordinated through Management and Legal. No personnel shall disclose incident details externally without authorization.
  • CERT-In shall be notified of reportable cyber security incidents within 6 hours of detection, as required by CERT-In directions (2022).
  • Affected data principals (individuals) shall be notified in accordance with the DPDP Act, 2023 requirements.

24.4 Incident Log

All incidents shall be logged in the Organization's incident tracking system with: incident ID, detection time, classification, affected systems, timeline of actions, responsible parties, resolution, and post-mortem reference. Incident records shall be retained for a minimum of 5 years.

25. Evidence Preservation

25.1 Chain of Custody

When a security incident requires evidence preservation, a formal chain of custody shall be established and maintained. The chain of custody log shall record: description of the evidence, date/time of collection, identity of the collecting individual, storage location, and every transfer of custody.

25.2 Forensic Procedures

  • Evidence shall be collected by trained personnel or a qualified external forensic specialist.
  • Original evidence shall be preserved in an unmodified state. Analysis shall be performed on forensic copies.
  • Forensic images shall be created using write-blocking tools and verified using cryptographic hashes (SHA-256).
  • Volatile evidence (memory, running processes, network connections) shall be captured before shutting down affected systems.
  • All forensic activities shall be documented in detail, including tools used, commands executed, and findings.

25.3 Evidence Storage

Digital evidence shall be stored on encrypted, access-controlled media. Physical evidence shall be stored in a secured, access-controlled location. Evidence shall be retained for the duration of any investigation or legal proceeding, plus an additional period as directed by Legal.

25.4 Law Enforcement Cooperation

Cooperation with law enforcement agencies shall be coordinated through the Organization's Legal counsel. Evidence shall be disclosed to law enforcement only through proper legal channels and with Legal approval. Compliance with orders from CERT-In and other authorized government agencies shall be ensured as required by the Information Technology Act, 2000.

  • CC1 (Control Environment): Sections 1, 2, 32, 35 — governance, roles, personnel security, training.
  • CC2 (Communication and Information): Sections 22, 23, 24 — logging, alerting, incident communication.
  • CC3 (Risk Assessment): Section 3 — risk management framework.
  • CC4 (Monitoring Activities): Sections 19, 22, 23, 42 — vulnerability management, monitoring, policy review.
  • CC5 (Control Activities): Sections 7–21 — access control, network security, change management, cryptography.
  • CC6 (Logical and Physical Access): Sections 7, 8, 9, 30, 38 — IAM, privileged access, authentication, physical security.
  • CC7 (System Operations): Sections 19, 20, 21, 22, 24 — vulnerability management, patching, malware, incident response.
  • CC8 (Change Management): Sections 13, 14, 15, 20 — SDLC, source code, patch management.
  • CC9 (Risk Mitigation): Sections 3, 29, 30, 39 — risk treatment, vendor risk, third-party access, exceptions.

40.1.3 ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Alignment

This Policy aligns with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls. The Organization does not claim ISO 27001 certification unless separately attested. Key Annex A control families addressed by this Policy include: A.5 (Organizational Controls), A.6 (People Controls), A.7 (Physical Controls), and A.8 (Technological Controls).

40.2 Internal Compliance Monitoring

The ISO shall conduct internal compliance reviews at least semi-annually to assess adherence to this Policy. Findings shall be documented, assigned remediation owners, and tracked to closure. Significant non-compliance shall be reported to the Information Security Committee and Management.

40.3 External Audits

The Organization shall undergo external security audits (e.g., SOC 2 Type II) as required by contractual obligations or management direction. Audit findings shall be remediated according to the agreed timelines, and the ISO shall track remediation progress.

41. Disciplinary Consequences

41.1 Violation Handling

Violations of this Policy may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment or contract, and legal action where applicable. The severity of disciplinary action shall be proportionate to the nature and impact of the violation.

41.2 Violation Categories

CategoryExamplesPotential Consequences
Minor / First-Time Failure to lock screen, minor clean desk violation, late completion of security training Verbal warning, mandatory remedial training
Moderate / Repeated Sharing credentials, installing unauthorized software, repeated failure of phishing simulations, unauthorized data storage Written warning, access restriction, mandatory retraining, performance impact
Severe / Intentional Unauthorized access to Restricted data, circumventing security controls, data theft, sabotage, failure to report a known incident Suspension, termination of employment/contract, legal action, reporting to authorities

41.3 Escalation

All suspected violations shall be reported to the ISO. The ISO shall investigate in coordination with HR and, where appropriate, Legal. Confirmed violations shall be documented and retained in the individual's personnel file. Violations involving potential criminal activity shall be reported to the appropriate Indian law enforcement authorities through the Organization's Legal counsel.

41.4 Non-Retaliation

The Organization prohibits retaliation against any individual who reports a security concern or policy violation in good faith. Reports may be made through the ISO, HR, or the Organization's designated reporting channel.

42. Policy Review

42.1 Review Cycle

This Policy shall be reviewed and, if necessary, updated at least annually. The annual review shall be coordinated by the ISO and approved by Management. The next scheduled review date shall be recorded in the document control table.

42.2 Triggers for Out-of-Cycle Review

This Policy shall be reviewed outside the annual cycle upon any of the following events:

  • A significant security incident.
  • Material changes in the Organization's business operations, technology environment, or organizational structure.
  • Changes in applicable laws, regulations, or regulatory guidance (e.g., new CERT-In directions, DPDP Act rules).
  • Findings from internal or external audits that indicate policy deficiencies.
  • Significant changes in the threat landscape.
  • Acquisition, merger, or significant partnership agreements.

42.3 Change Management

All changes to this Policy shall be documented in the version history table. Changes shall be reviewed by the Information Security Committee and approved by Management before publication. All personnel shall be notified of material changes and required to review the updated Policy.
