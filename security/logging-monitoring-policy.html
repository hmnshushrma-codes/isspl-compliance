2. Purpose

This policy defines the requirements for logging security-relevant events and monitoring information systems at Ramdoot Solutions. Effective logging and monitoring enable the organization to detect security incidents, support forensic investigations, meet regulatory obligations, and maintain operational visibility across all technology environments.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

  • All production, staging, and disaster-recovery information systems, including servers, databases, applications, and network devices.
  • Cloud-hosted infrastructure and services under Ramdoot Solutions' operational control.
  • Security systems including firewalls, intrusion detection/prevention systems, VPN concentrators, and endpoint protection platforms.
  • Identity and access management systems.
  • All personnel responsible for system administration, security operations, and incident response.

4. Objectives

  1. Ensure all security-relevant events are captured in logs with sufficient detail for detection, investigation, and compliance purposes.
  2. Establish centralized log collection and correlation to enable timely detection of suspicious activity.
  3. Protect log integrity to ensure logs are reliable for forensic and audit purposes.
  4. Define log retention periods that satisfy regulatory, contractual, and operational requirements.
  5. Implement monitoring dashboards and alerting to enable proactive threat detection and response.

5. Definitions

7. Policy Statements

  1. All in-scope systems shall generate and retain logs of security-relevant events as defined in Section 8.
  2. Logs from all systems must be forwarded to a centralized logging and SIEM platform.
  3. Log data shall be retained for a minimum of twelve (12) months online and accessible, with extended archival as required by regulation or contract.
  4. Log integrity must be protected through write-once storage, cryptographic hashing, or equivalent controls to prevent tampering.
  5. Monitoring dashboards and automated alerting shall be implemented to enable real-time and near-real-time detection of security threats.
  6. All system clocks must be synchronized to a common, authoritative time source (NTP) to ensure log correlation accuracy.
  7. Logs must not contain plaintext passwords, authentication tokens, encryption keys, or other sensitive credentials.
  8. Access to log data shall be restricted to authorized personnel on a need-to-know basis.
  9. Logging configurations must not be disabled or modified without change management approval.

8. Detailed Requirements

8.1 Events That Must Be Logged

The following categories of events shall be logged across all in-scope systems:

8.1.1 Authentication Events

  • Successful and failed login attempts (including source IP, username, timestamp).
  • Account lockouts and unlocks.
  • Password changes and resets.
  • Multi-factor authentication success and failure.
  • Session creation, termination, and timeout.
  • Single Sign-On (SSO) and federated authentication events.

8.1.2 Access Control Changes

  • User account creation, modification, and deletion.
  • Role and permission assignments and revocations.
  • Group membership changes.
  • Access control list (ACL) modifications.
  • Privilege escalation events.

8.1.3 Administrative Actions

  • System configuration changes.
  • Security policy changes.
  • Firewall rule modifications.
  • Service start, stop, and restart events.
  • Scheduled task and cron job creation or modification.
  • Software installation and removal.
  • Backup and restore operations.

8.1.4 Error and Exception Events

  • Application errors and unhandled exceptions.
  • System errors and warnings.
  • Resource exhaustion events (disk, memory, CPU).
  • Service failures and crash events.
  • Certificate expiration warnings and errors.

8.1.5 Data Access Events

  • Access to sensitive or classified data repositories.
  • Database queries involving personally identifiable information (PII) or financial data.
  • File access events on sensitive file shares and document management systems.
  • Data export and download events.
  • API calls to sensitive endpoints.

8.3 Centralized Logging Infrastructure

  • All in-scope systems shall forward logs to the centralized SIEM/log management platform: [TO BE COMPLETED BY RAMDOOT SOLUTIONS].
  • Log transmission must use encrypted channels (e.g., TLS) to protect log data in transit.
  • The centralized platform shall support ingestion from diverse log sources including syslog, Windows Event Logs, application logs, cloud service logs, and API-based log sources.
  • Log parsing and normalization shall be configured to enable consistent querying and correlation across all log sources.
  • Redundancy and high availability shall be implemented for the centralized logging infrastructure to prevent log loss.

8.4 Log Integrity

  • Logs stored in the centralized platform shall be protected against unauthorized modification or deletion.
  • Write-once storage, cryptographic hashing, or digital signatures shall be used to ensure log integrity.
  • Access to modify or delete log data must be restricted to a minimal set of authorized administrators, with all such actions logged separately.
  • Log integrity verification checks shall be performed at least quarterly.

8.5 Monitoring Dashboards

The IT Security Team shall maintain monitoring dashboards that provide visibility into:

  • Authentication activity (login volumes, failed login trends, geographic anomalies).
  • Privileged account usage.
  • Firewall and network security events.
  • Vulnerability scan findings and remediation status.
  • System health and resource utilization.
  • Data access patterns and anomalies.

8.6 Alerting Thresholds

Automated alerts shall be configured for the following conditions (at minimum):

Alert ConditionSeverityResponse Target
Multiple failed login attempts (5+ within 10 minutes for a single account)MediumInvestigate within 1 hour
Successful login from a new geographic location for a privileged accountHighInvestigate within 30 minutes
Privilege escalation outside of change windowCriticalInvestigate within 15 minutes
Firewall rule change outside of change windowCriticalInvestigate within 15 minutes
Log forwarding failure from any sourceHighInvestigate within 1 hour
Mass data download or export exceeding baseline thresholdsHighInvestigate within 30 minutes
System service stopped unexpectedlyMediumInvestigate within 1 hour
Malware detection alert from endpoint protectionHighInvestigate within 30 minutes

Alerting thresholds shall be tuned regularly to reduce false positives while maintaining detection effectiveness.

9. Exceptions

Exceptions to this policy must be requested in writing and approved by the CISO. Systems that cannot meet logging requirements due to technical limitations shall have compensating controls documented and approved. All exceptions shall be recorded and reviewed at least annually.

10. Monitoring & Compliance

  • The IT Security Team shall review logging coverage and completeness on a monthly basis.
  • Log integrity verification shall be performed quarterly.
  • Internal Audit shall assess logging and monitoring effectiveness at least annually.
  • Key metrics:
    • Percentage of in-scope systems forwarding logs to the centralized platform.
    • Mean time to detect (MTTD) for security events.
    • Alert volume and false positive rate.
    • Log retention compliance rate.
    • Number of log integrity verification failures.

11. Violations & Enforcement

  • Disabling or modifying logging configurations without authorization shall be treated as a security incident.
  • Tampering with or deleting log data shall be treated as a serious policy violation and may result in termination and legal action.
  • Failure to configure required logging on new systems shall result in the system being blocked from production deployment.
  • Non-compliant systems may be isolated from the network until logging requirements are met.

15. Review Cycle

This policy shall be reviewed at least annually, or sooner if triggered by:

  • Security incidents where logging gaps were identified.
  • Changes to regulatory or contractual logging requirements.
  • Significant changes to the technology environment or SIEM platform.
  • Findings from internal or external audits.
