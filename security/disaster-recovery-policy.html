2. Purpose

This policy establishes the requirements for disaster recovery (DR) planning, testing, and execution at Ramdoot Solutions. It ensures that critical information systems and technology infrastructure can be recovered within defined timeframes following a major disruption or disaster, minimizing data loss and enabling the organization to resume normal operations. This policy operates in conjunction with the Business Continuity Policy (RS-POL-015) and provides the technology-focused recovery component of the broader business continuity framework.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

  • All production information systems, including servers, databases, applications, and network infrastructure.
  • Cloud-hosted services and infrastructure under Ramdoot Solutions' operational control.
  • Data storage systems, backup infrastructure, and replication mechanisms.
  • Communication systems required for DR coordination.
  • All personnel involved in DR planning, testing, and execution, including IT Operations, IT Security, application teams, and designated DR coordinators.

4. Objectives

  1. Ensure recovery of critical information systems within defined Recovery Time Objectives (RTO).
  2. Limit data loss to within defined Recovery Point Objectives (RPO).
  3. Maintain documented, tested, and current DR procedures for all critical systems.
  4. Establish recovery site capabilities that support failover and sustained operations.
  5. Test DR plans regularly and incorporate lessons learned to improve recovery capabilities.
  6. Define clear procedures for communication, failover, failback, and return to normal operations.

5. Definitions

TermDefinition
DisasterAn event that causes significant disruption to IT services, rendering the primary site or systems unable to function for an extended period, requiring activation of DR procedures.
Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)A documented set of procedures to recover and restore IT systems and data following a disaster.
Recovery SiteAn alternate location or environment where IT systems can be recovered and operated during and after a disaster.
Hot SiteA fully equipped recovery site with real-time or near-real-time data replication, capable of immediate failover.
Warm SiteA recovery site with hardware infrastructure provisioned but requiring data restoration and configuration before use.
Cold SiteA recovery site with basic infrastructure (power, cooling, connectivity) but requiring full system and data deployment.
FailoverThe process of switching operations from the primary site/system to the recovery site/system.
FailbackThe process of returning operations from the recovery site/system to the restored primary site/system.
Data ReplicationThe continuous or scheduled copying of data from the primary site to the recovery site to maintain data currency.

7. Policy Statements

  1. Ramdoot Solutions shall maintain a documented Disaster Recovery Plan for all critical information systems.
  2. DR plans must be aligned with the RTO and RPO values defined in the Business Impact Analysis.
  3. Data backup and replication shall be implemented to ensure data recovery within defined RPOs.
  4. A recovery site must be maintained with sufficient capacity to run critical systems during a disaster.
  5. DR plans shall be tested at least annually, with testing scope and methodology appropriate to the criticality of the systems involved.
  6. Failover and failback procedures must be documented, tested, and validated.
  7. Communication procedures during DR events shall be documented and include internal teams, management, customers, and service providers.
  8. A post-DR review shall be conducted after every DR invocation and test to capture lessons learned and drive improvements.
  9. DR documentation must be updated within 30 days of any significant infrastructure change.

8.2 Recovery Site Requirements

  • The recovery site shall be geographically separated from the primary site by a minimum distance sufficient to avoid common-cause disasters: [TO BE COMPLETED BY RAMDOOT SOLUTIONS].
  • The recovery site must have:
    • Sufficient compute, storage, and network capacity to run all Tier 1 and Tier 2 systems simultaneously.
    • Redundant power supply (UPS and generator backup).
    • Redundant network connectivity from at least two independent ISPs.
    • Physical security controls equivalent to the primary site.
    • Environmental controls (cooling, fire suppression) appropriate for the equipment hosted.
  • For cloud-based recovery sites, the cloud region shall be in a different geographic zone from the primary region.
  • Recovery site readiness shall be verified at least quarterly.

8.3 Failover Procedures

  1. DR Activation Decision: The CISO or IT Director, in consultation with the Crisis Management Team, determines that a disaster has occurred and authorizes DR activation.
  2. Notification: The DR Coordinator notifies all DR team members and relevant stakeholders via the communication plan.
  3. Assessment: The DR team assesses the scope of the disaster and identifies affected systems.
  4. Failover Execution:
    • DNS changes are executed to redirect traffic to the recovery site.
    • Database failover is initiated to the replicated standby databases.
    • Application services are started on the recovery infrastructure.
    • Network configurations are updated to route traffic appropriately.
    • Load balancer configurations are adjusted for the recovery environment.
  5. Validation: Application teams validate functionality, data integrity, and connectivity on the recovery site.
  6. Declaration: The DR Coordinator declares successful failover and notifies stakeholders.

8.4 DR Testing Schedule

Test TypeFrequencyScope
Tabletop Exercise Bi-annually Walk-through of DR procedures with all DR team members; validate communication plan and decision-making processes.
Component Test Quarterly Test individual DR components: backup restoration, database failover, network failover, application recovery.
Full Failover Test Annually Complete failover of Tier 1 systems to the recovery site; validate RTO/RPO achievement; run production workloads from DR site for a defined period.

8.5 Data Replication

  • Data replication shall be implemented for all Tier 1 and Tier 2 systems to the recovery site.
  • Replication method and frequency must be consistent with the RPO defined for each system tier.
  • Replication health shall be monitored continuously, with alerts configured for replication lag or failure.
  • Data integrity checks shall be performed on replicated data at least monthly to verify consistency with the primary site.
  • Backup copies shall also be maintained independently of replication to protect against logical corruption propagation.
  • Backup testing (restore verification) shall be performed at least quarterly for Tier 1 systems and semi-annually for Tier 2/3 systems.

8.7 Return to Normal Operations

  1. Before failback, the primary site must be fully restored and validated as operational, including:
    • Infrastructure hardware and network connectivity verified.
    • Operating systems and applications reinstalled/restored and patched to current levels.
    • Security controls verified and operational.
    • Data synchronization from DR site to primary site completed and verified.
  2. Failback shall be scheduled during a maintenance window to minimize service disruption.
  3. The failback procedure mirrors the failover process in reverse: DNS redirection, database failback, application restart at primary, network reconfiguration.
  4. Post-failback validation must confirm that all systems are operational, data is consistent, and performance is normal.
  5. Enhanced monitoring shall be maintained for 72 hours following failback.

8.8 Post-DR Review

  • A Post-DR Review shall be conducted within ten (10) business days of completing DR operations (whether test or actual invocation).
  • The review must document:
    • Timeline of events from disaster detection through return to normal.
    • Actual RTO and RPO achieved versus defined targets.
    • Issues encountered during failover, DR operations, and failback.
    • Communication effectiveness.
    • Corrective actions with owners and target dates.
    • Recommendations for DR plan improvements.
  • Corrective actions shall be tracked to closure and reported to management.

9. Exceptions

Exceptions to DR requirements must be approved by the CISO and documented with a risk assessment, compensating controls, and a review date not exceeding 12 months. Systems without DR capability shall be flagged as a risk in the organizational risk register.

10. Monitoring & Compliance

  • DR readiness status shall be reported to the CISO monthly and to senior management quarterly.
  • Data replication health shall be monitored continuously with automated alerting.
  • Backup success rates shall be reported weekly.
  • Internal Audit shall review DR program effectiveness at least annually.
  • Key metrics:
    • DR test completion rate (annual target: 100%).
    • RTO/RPO achievement rate during tests.
    • Backup success rate (target: 99.5%).
    • Replication lag (target: within RPO).
    • Corrective action closure rate from DR reviews.
    • DR plan currency (last update date).

11. Violations & Enforcement

  • Failure to maintain current DR documentation shall result in escalation to the CISO.
  • Systems deployed to production without documented DR plans shall be flagged as non-compliant and the risk recorded.
  • Failure to participate in or complete DR testing shall be escalated to management.
  • Personnel who disable or interfere with backup or replication processes without authorization may face disciplinary action.

15. Review Cycle

This policy shall be reviewed at least annually, or sooner if triggered by:

  • Actual DR invocation (test or real event).
  • Significant infrastructure changes (e.g., cloud migration, data center relocation, new critical systems).
  • Findings from DR tests or internal/external audits.
  • Changes to RTO/RPO requirements from updated BIA.
  • Changes in regulatory or contractual requirements.
