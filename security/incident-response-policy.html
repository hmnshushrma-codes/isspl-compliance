2. Purpose

This policy establishes the framework for detecting, reporting, assessing, responding to, and recovering from information security incidents at Ramdoot Solutions. It defines the incident classification methodology, response team structure, escalation paths, communication protocols, and regulatory notification obligations (including CERT-In requirements) to ensure a coordinated and effective response that minimizes damage and supports rapid recovery.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

  • All information security incidents affecting Ramdoot Solutions' systems, data, networks, or personnel.
  • All production, staging, development, and disaster-recovery environments.
  • Incidents involving third-party service providers that process or store Ramdoot Solutions data.
  • All employees, contractors, consultants, and temporary personnel.
  • Physical security incidents that may impact information assets.

4. Objectives

  1. Detect and report security incidents promptly through defined channels.
  2. Classify incidents accurately to ensure appropriate response effort and escalation.
  3. Contain incidents rapidly to prevent further damage.
  4. Eradicate the root cause and recover affected systems to normal operations.
  5. Preserve evidence for forensic analysis and potential legal proceedings.
  6. Comply with regulatory notification requirements, including CERT-In's 6-hour reporting mandate.
  7. Conduct post-incident reviews to identify improvements and prevent recurrence.

5. Definitions

TermDefinition
Security IncidentAn event that actually or potentially jeopardizes the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an information system or the information it processes, stores, or transmits, or constitutes a violation of security policies or procedures.

Incident Response Team (IRT)The cross-functional team responsible for managing the response to security incidents.
ContainmentActions taken to limit the scope and impact of a security incident.
EradicationThe process of eliminating the root cause of a security incident from the environment.
CERT-InIndian Computer Emergency Response Team — the national agency for responding to computer security incidents in India.
Chain of CustodyThe documented and unbroken transfer of evidence from collection to presentation, maintaining evidence integrity.
Post-Incident Review (PIR)A structured review conducted after incident closure to identify root causes, lessons learned, and improvement actions.

7. Policy Statements

  1. Ramdoot Solutions shall maintain a documented Incident Response Plan that is tested at least annually.
  2. All security incidents must be reported to the IT Security Team within one (1) hour of detection.
  3. Incidents shall be classified using the priority system defined in Section 8.1 (P1 through P4).
  4. The Incident Response Team must be activated for all P1 and P2 incidents.
  5. CERT-In shall be notified within six (6) hours of discovery for incidents meeting the mandatory reporting criteria as per the Information Technology (The Indian Computer Emergency Response Team and Manner of Performing Functions and Duties) Rules, 2013 and subsequent directives.
  6. Evidence must be collected, preserved, and handled following chain-of-custody procedures.
  7. A Post-Incident Review shall be conducted for all P1 and P2 incidents, and optionally for P3 incidents.
  8. Incident response capabilities shall be tested through tabletop exercises or simulations at least annually.

8. Detailed Requirements

8.1 Incident Classification

PrioritySeverityDescriptionExamplesResponse Time
P1 Critical Confirmed breach of sensitive data; complete service outage; active exploitation with widespread impact. Data breach involving PII/financial data; ransomware attack; complete infrastructure compromise. Immediate (within 15 minutes)
P2 High Significant security event with potential for data loss or service disruption; active threat requiring urgent response. Malware outbreak on multiple systems; unauthorized access to production systems; significant DDoS attack. Within 1 hour
P3 Medium Security event with limited scope; potential threat that is contained or under investigation. Phishing campaign targeting employees; single system compromise; unauthorized access attempt blocked. Within 4 hours
P4 Low Minor security event; policy violation; informational security alert with no immediate impact. Single failed social engineering attempt; minor policy violation; informational scanning activity. Within 24 hours

8.2 Incident Response Phases

8.2.1 Detection and Reporting

  • Security incidents may be detected through automated monitoring (SIEM alerts, IDS/IPS, endpoint protection), user reports, third-party notifications, or threat intelligence feeds.
  • All personnel shall report suspected incidents to the IT Security Team via email ([TO BE COMPLETED BY RAMDOOT SOLUTIONS]) or phone ([TO BE COMPLETED BY RAMDOOT SOLUTIONS]).
  • The IT Security Team shall perform initial triage within the response times defined in Section 8.1.

8.2.2 Containment

  • Short-term containment shall be implemented immediately to limit incident scope (e.g., isolating affected systems, blocking malicious IPs, disabling compromised accounts).
  • Long-term containment shall be implemented to allow continued operations while eradication is planned (e.g., deploying temporary fixes, increasing monitoring on affected systems).
  • Containment decisions shall balance the need to preserve evidence with the need to limit damage.
  • The Incident Commander shall authorize containment actions for P1 and P2 incidents.

8.2.3 Eradication

  • Root cause analysis shall be performed to identify the underlying vulnerability or attack vector.
  • The threat must be completely removed from the environment (e.g., removing malware, patching vulnerabilities, closing unauthorized access paths).
  • All compromised credentials shall be reset.
  • Affected systems shall be rebuilt or restored from known-clean backups where complete eradication cannot be verified.

8.2.4 Recovery

  • Affected systems shall be restored to normal operations following a phased approach.
  • Systems must be validated as clean and properly configured before being returned to production.
  • Enhanced monitoring shall be maintained on recovered systems for a minimum of 30 days to detect any recurrence.
  • Users and business units shall be notified when services are restored.

8.4 Regulatory Notification — CERT-In

In accordance with the directions issued by CERT-In under the Information Technology Act, 2000, Ramdoot Solutions shall report the following types of incidents within six (6) hours of noticing or being brought to notice:

  • Targeted scanning/probing of critical networks/systems.
  • Compromise of critical systems/information.
  • Unauthorized access of IT systems/data.
  • Defacement of websites or intrusion into websites.
  • Malicious code attacks such as spreading of virus/worm/Trojan/botnets/spyware/ransomware/cryptominers.
  • Attack on servers (database, mail, DNS) and network devices (routers).
  • Identity theft, spoofing and phishing attacks.
  • Denial of Service (DoS) and Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks.
  • Attacks on critical infrastructure, SCADA, and operational technology systems and wireless networks.
  • Attacks on applications such as e-governance, e-commerce, etc.
  • Data breach or data leak.
  • Attacks or incidents affecting cloud computing systems/servers/software/applications.
  • Attacks or malicious/suspicious activities affecting systems/servers/networks/software/applications related to Big Data, Blockchain, virtual assets, virtual asset exchanges, custodian wallets, robotics, 3D and 4D printing, additive manufacturing, and drones.
  • Attacks or malicious/suspicious activities affecting systems/servers/software/applications related to Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning.

The CISO shall ensure that incident notification procedures and contact details for CERT-In are current and readily accessible.

8.5 Post-Incident Review

  • A Post-Incident Review (PIR) shall be conducted within ten (10) business days of incident closure for all P1 and P2 incidents.
  • The PIR must document:
    • Incident timeline — from detection through closure.
    • Root cause analysis.
    • Effectiveness of response actions.
    • Gaps identified in detection, response, or communication.
    • Corrective actions with owners and target dates.
    • Recommendations for preventing recurrence.
  • PIR findings shall be tracked in the organization's corrective action register and reported to senior management.

8.6 Evidence Preservation

  • All digital evidence shall be collected and preserved following forensically sound procedures.
  • Chain of custody must be maintained for all evidence, documenting who collected it, when, where, and how it was stored.
  • Evidence shall include (as applicable): system logs, memory dumps, disk images, network captures, malware samples, and screenshots.
  • Evidence storage must be secure, with access restricted to authorized incident response personnel and legal counsel.
  • Evidence shall be retained for the duration of any investigation, legal proceedings, or regulatory inquiry, and for a minimum of three (3) years after incident closure.

9. Exceptions

No exceptions to CERT-In notification requirements are permitted. Exceptions to other provisions of this policy must be approved in writing by the CISO and documented in the exception register.

10. Monitoring & Compliance

  • Incident response metrics shall be reported to the CISO monthly and to senior leadership quarterly.
  • Incident response plan testing shall be conducted at least annually through tabletop exercises or simulations.
  • Key metrics:
    • Mean time to detect (MTTD).
    • Mean time to respond (MTTR).
    • Mean time to contain (MTTC).
    • Number of incidents by classification (P1-P4).
    • CERT-In notification compliance rate (within 6 hours).
    • PIR completion rate for P1/P2 incidents.
    • Number and status of corrective actions from PIRs.

11. Violations & Enforcement

  • Failure to report a suspected security incident within the required timeframe may result in disciplinary action.
  • Deliberately concealing or destroying evidence related to a security incident shall be treated as gross misconduct.
  • Failure to comply with CERT-In notification requirements may result in legal penalties under the Information Technology Act, 2000.
  • Personnel who fail to cooperate with incident investigations may face disciplinary action, up to and including termination.

15. Review Cycle

This policy shall be reviewed at least annually, or sooner if triggered by:

  • Major security incidents that revealed gaps in the response process.
  • Changes to CERT-In reporting requirements or other regulatory obligations.
  • Organizational changes affecting the Incident Response Team structure.
  • Findings from incident response plan tests or external audits.
